# Product brief: Give the reviewer the ticket's Out of scope: a finding about out-of-scope work is never blocking (C10, C1)

- Source: issue #134 (https://github.com/plaplanche/ariane/issues/134)

## Issue

Prefilled from the issue title and body (never its comments).

## Context

Every ticket's body has an "Out of scope" section (C1). In the benchmark on a reference project, a reviewer raised blocking findings about work that the ticket's Out of scope assigned to later tickets; the implementer correctly did not do that work, the two fix rounds of C10 ran out, and the ticket ended as a draft pull request handed to a human, though the change was what the ticket asked. The reviewer sees the issue body but has no rule about scope, and the fix session is not told to leave out-of-scope work alone. Spec: C10 (verdict and findings), C1 (issue format), C26 (definition of done).

## Current state

- `src/ariane/review.py:208-291`: `prompt` puts the whole issue body in an untrusted block; its rules (`review.py:262-270`) say "Check that the change does what the issue asks" and nothing about scope.
- `src/ariane/review.py:168-206`: `settle` makes the verdict `no-go` for any blocking finding.
- `src/ariane/context.py:65-84`: the implementer's prompt (also used by fix rounds, with the findings, `context.py:55-64`) has no rule about the Out of scope section.
- No code reads a section of the issue body: nothing in `src/ariane/` parses `## Out of scope`.

## Decided spec

- New function `ticket.section(body, title) -> str`: the text under the level-2 heading `## <title>` (title matched without case, surrounding spaces ignored) up to the next level-2 heading or the end; empty when absent.
- **Reviewer prompt.** When the issue's `Out of scope` section is not empty, the prompt shows it again as its own untrusted block (`kind="out-of-scope"`), and the rules gain: "Work listed under Out of scope belongs to other tickets. A finding about that work, or one asking for it, is never blocking: report it as minor, with "out of scope" in its title, or leave it out. Doing out-of-scope work is a finding only when it breaks something."
- **Implementer and fix prompts.** The rules gain: "Do not do what the issue lists under Out of scope, even when a finding asks for it; say so in your summary."
- Ariane does not decide in code whether a finding is out of scope (it cannot without a model): this is a framed opinion, and the verdict rules of `settle` are unchanged.
- No ADR: the spec already says what is in a ticket; this only tells the agents.

## Release note

Changed: the reviewer and the fix sessions are told the ticket's Out of scope, and a finding about out-of-scope work is never blocking.
